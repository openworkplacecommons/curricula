# Accessibility, Localization and Licensing

## Accessibility from the source

- Use meaningful heading hierarchy, concise language, descriptive links, alt text for informative images, transcripts for audio and captions for video.
- Learning outcomes should not require color, sound or fine motor actions as the only means of completing an activity.
- Exporters and portal components should support keyboard operation, screen readers, visible focus and suitable contrast.
- Use WCAG 2.2 as a web accessibility design/reference standard; an OWC document alone cannot certify an individual player or LMS as compliant.
- Flag inaccessible external media or provide a usable alternative; record known gaps in review metadata.

## Localization and jurisdictions

- Every resource has a language tag and can be linked to a source version. Translated resources must be attributed and reviewed.
- Distinguish **translation** (same intended claims) from **regional adaptation** (different laws, examples, contacts or workplace requirements).
- An example on data privacy must not silently claim that one country's regulations govern all adopters.
- Company-specific policies (approved AI tools, incident contacts, HR reporting routes) belong in employer overlays, not in the global baseline.

## License and provenance rules (to ratify)

- Proposed starting point: **CC BY 4.0** for original educational text and question content; verify suitability and contributors' rights before adoption.
- Choose an explicit code license (e.g., MIT or Apache-2.0) *separately* for validators/exporters; do not assume the content license covers code.
- A public URL, citation or embedded link is not permission to redistribute a third-party asset. Record per-asset licenses and permissions.
- Contributor terms should say what license each submission will be published under and whether commercial reuse/adaptation is permitted.
- Review potential attribution and trademarks separately; reuse must not imply OWC endorsement or accreditation.

**Open decision:** finalize licenses, the contributor rights declaration/DCO vs CLA policy, and the way licensing metadata is inherited from course to resource to asset. Do not advertise a license as final until approved.
